Hong Wang

I'm a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at Boston University. My research is in economic history and political economy, with secondary interests in gender economics and media economics. I will be on the job market in 2026-2027.

Beginning in August 2026, I will be the Mary Beth and Chris Gordon Postdoctoral Fellow in Gender Inequality in the Department of Economics at Harvard University.

My work studies how cultural and social entrepreneurs, media, and organizational infrastructure shape public opinion, legislative behavior, and political mobilization.

Research

Job Market Paper

What Killed the ERA? The Role of Entrepreneurs in the Marketplace of Ideas and Policy

FM moved votes. Television moved language. Using the physics of broadcast reception, I show how Phyllis Schlafly’s CBS platform reshaped the political fight over the ERA.

Working Papers

A Choice, Not an Echo: Cultural Matching in the Marketplace of Ideas and Policy

Institutions can create the very constituencies that later defend them. But interests do not become politics on their own: someone has to find the old argument that fits.

Grounded: Radio Propagation, Soil Geochemistry, and Political AM Radio

Better physics can change an empirical result without making it more causal. Rebuilding 1930s AM reception with ground-wave propagation overturns the U.S. association while leaving the identification problem intact.

A Republic, Not a Democracy: The John Birch Society and the Geography of the American Right

The John Birch Society left behind a map. Forty years later, that map looks less like a genealogy of Trumpism than a record of how a movement disappeared into the Republican Party.
